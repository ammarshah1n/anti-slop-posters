# Copy

Words are most of what makes a poster read as human or as machine. Machine copy is
long, abstract, and enthusiastic. Human poster copy is short, concrete and calm.

## Rules

1. **Headline: at most seven words.** It is a label for the thing, not a sentence
   about the thing. "Salt Flats" beats "Experience the Raw Energy of Salt Flats Live".
2. **Lead with what it is, then when, then where.** In that order.
3. **Specifics, not adjectives.** "Eleven photographers" beats "a stunning collection".
   "8 to 1" beats "all morning". "Free entry" beats "accessible to all".
4. **One call to action.** A URL or a QR code, not both, not five social handles.
5. **Only facts you were given.** Never invent a date, address, price, quote,
   testimonial, award or sponsor. If a fact is missing, write `[TO CONFIRM]` and ask.
6. **Short dates and times.** "Fri 14 Mar, doors 7pm". One format everywhere. 24-hour
   or am/pm, not both.
7. **Sentence case.** Not Title Case On Every Word. No ALL CAPS paragraphs.
8. **Plain punctuation.** No em dashes. No exclamation marks. No ellipses for drama.

## Banned phrases

elevate, unleash, unlock, reimagined, seamless, game-changer, cutting-edge,
next-level, world-class, revolutionise, unforgettable, immersive, curated experience,
"discover the future of", "join us for", "where X meets Y", "more than just a",
"whether you're a... or a...", "something for everyone", "don't miss out".

## The test

Read the poster text aloud as if to a friend in a pub. If you would be embarrassed
to say it, a person would be embarrassed to read it.
