# The brief (fill this in, 2 minutes)

Most bad posters come from a bad brief. "Make a cool poster for my event" gets you
the average of every poster the model has seen. Fill this in, paste it after the
master prompt, and the output gets better before any design happens.

```
WHAT IT IS:
  (one sentence: "A Friday night gig at a small bar", "An open day at my school")

WHO SEES IT, WHERE:
  (Instagram feed / printed on a wall at A3 / on a shop window / emailed PDF)

FORMAT:
  (1080x1350 social, 1080x1920 story, A3, A4, 18x24 in, other)

EXACT COPY (only what must appear; I will not invent anything):
  Headline:
  Date and time:
  Venue and address:
  Price / entry:
  One call to action (URL or QR, not both):
  Names / logos that must appear:

MOOD:
  Three words:
  One reference I like (a poster, album cover, brand, magazine): 
  One thing I hate:

DIRECTION:
  (pick 1-8 from the master prompt, or "you choose")

IMAGE:
  (I will supply a photo / no image, type only / generate one for me)

ANYTHING ELSE I SHOULD KNOW:
```

## If you only have 30 seconds

Write three lines: what it is, the exact date/venue/price, and one poster you
like. The master prompt will assume the rest and tell you what it assumed.

## Why the reference matters

A named reference ("a 1970s Polish film poster", "Blue Note album covers", "the Tate
signage") steers the model off the default more than any adjective. It cannot
copy it, so it borrows the structure. Always give one.
